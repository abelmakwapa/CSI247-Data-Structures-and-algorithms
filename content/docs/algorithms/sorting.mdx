## Definition and intuition

<AcademicPanel tone="definition" title="Pay once to create order">
Sorting repeatedly compares or places values until the sequence follows an order. Ask about time, space, stability, and whether comparison is the only available information.
</AcademicPanel>

## The problem it solves

Sorting makes duplicate detection, ranking, interval scheduling, two-pointer scans, and ordered search easier to express.

## Real-world analogy

Arrange books by height once and later queries can scan a predictable shelf instead of a random pile.

## Trade-offs

- Ordering simplifies many later algorithms.
- Sorting may be unnecessary work, and stability or in-place behavior depends on the algorithm and runtime.

## Common mistakes

<AcademicPanel tone="warning" title="Do not stop at O(n log n)">
State whether the sort is stable, what its auxiliary space is, and whether the input constraints allow counting or radix-style methods.
</AcademicPanel>

## Interview questions

- Why can sorting expose duplicates?
- What is the comparison-sort lower bound?
- When is a simple O(n²) sort acceptable?
