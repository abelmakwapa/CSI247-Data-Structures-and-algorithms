## Definition and intuition

<AcademicPanel tone="definition" title="Keep a valid interval">
Compare the target with the midpoint, discard the impossible half, and preserve an invariant that the answer—if it exists—remains inside the interval.
</AcademicPanel>

## The problem it solves

Binary search finds values in sorted arrays and answers monotonic “first feasible” or “minimum valid” questions in logarithmic iterations.

## Real-world analogy

Guess a number by asking “higher or lower?” at the midpoint, never reopening a half already ruled out.

## Trade-offs

- Logarithmic search is powerful on large ordered ranges.
- Unsorted input breaks the discard proof, and boundary conventions create off-by-one bugs.

## Common mistakes

<AcademicPanel tone="warning" title="Write the invariant before the loop">
Decide whether your interval is closed or half-open, then test empty input, one item, endpoints, and missing values.
</AcademicPanel>

## Interview questions

- What exactly does the interval invariant say?
- How do you find a lower bound or first feasible answer?
- When does sorting cost more than it saves?
